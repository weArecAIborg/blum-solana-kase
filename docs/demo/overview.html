bond_servicing · Solana devnet

Technical overview

Corporate actions for tokenized bonds on Solana: coupon payment, redemption at maturity, and an issuer buyback and tender offer. Program bond_servicing, id 2UkTKYYzgZXZsganCqfxyY5Q5EeynUSBbErdKMpy8sHf, deployed on devnet.

The system services any bond: every market-specific value (record day and time, payment time, time zone, weekend, holiday calendar, coupon basis) is a bond parameter. The sample bond is the KASE listing's example (B1: face 1,000, 10%, semi-annual, 2 years) with the Kazakh depository's rules: record list at 09:00 Almaty on the payment day, Saturday/Sunday weekend, Kazakh holiday calendar.

Detail: bond-processing.md (every instruction, every guarantee and its test), buyback-tender.md (third action).

Off-chain parts hold no rights and no keys that matter for payments: amounts and eligibility are decided on chain.

2. Record-date logic

The rule. A coupon belongs to whoever held the bond at the record moment, whatever they hold when the money arrives. Trading stays open on coupon day; it closes at the final record moment for redemption.

Record moment = record day + record time (bond-local, with the bond's UTC offset).

  • The record day is N business days before the payment day (N = 0 for the sample: 09:00 on the payment day).
  • Payment and record days are computed off-chain from the terms and the bond's holiday calendar (bond_math::schedule). create_bond does not trust them: it recomputes the regular schedule and checks each day (within 14 days of its unadjusted date, not on the bond's weekend, record moments increasing, record not after payment).
  • The program reads the chain clock (Clock sysvar) and converts it to bond time: bond time = bond origin + (chain time - chain origin) x 86400 / chain seconds per bond day. Real time is 86400; devnet demos compress a two-year bond into minutes or hours.

Positions at the record moment, without a snapshot. A program sees only the accounts passed to a transaction, so it cannot list all holders at 09:00. Instead, every balance change saves the holder's quantity first:

  • HolderRecord.position (PositionHistory): the current quantity, how many record moments had passed at the last change, and one saved quantity per record moment (up to 40);
  • before each change, the quantity is written for every record moment passed since the previous change;
  • quantity at record moment e = saved value if e passed before the last change, else the current quantity.

Between two changes a quantity is constant, so the next change saves exactly the quantity each passed record moment had. Nothing expires; nobody has to act at the record moment.

Every balance change goes through the program.

  • Transfers: Token-2022 calls the program's transfer hook in the same transaction. The hook requires registered holders on both sides (associated token accounts), checks the record matches the token balance, saves history, refuses transfers below a tendered (locked) quantity, and refuses all transfers from the final record moment on.
  • Placement, redemption, buyback and tender settlement are program instructions.
  • Holders cannot burn: the mint's permissioned burn makes Token-2022 refuse any burn not signed by the Bond PDA.

The bond's supply has the same history (Bond.supply), so an event's budget uses the supply at its record moment.

3. Entitlement calculations

All integer arithmetic in u128, rounded down, overflow reported as an error (bond_servicing/src/state.rs, bond_math).

Amount Formula
Coupon of a holder for period e position at record moment e x face x rate / periods per year (basis FixedPerPeriod), or by 30/360 (Bond Basis, European, USA) on the period's dates; rounded down once on the total position
Principal at maturity position x face x redemption price; partial redemptions use principal(redeemed + now) - principal(redeemed), so they round exactly like one redemption
Event budget the same formula on the supply at the record moment, rounded down once; the sum of holder amounts never exceeds it, and any residue stays visible in that event's vault
Buyback price per bond x quantity sold, first come first served up to the offer maximum
Tender everything tendered if the total fits the maximum; else tenders are resolved in the order they were made, each accepting floor(running total x maximum / total tendered) minus what the earlier ones accepted, so an oversubscribed offer buys exactly its maximum; paid at the offer price

Sample (the listing's example): 10 bonds at the record moment -> coupon 10 x 1,000 x 10% / 2 = 500. At maturity: 10,000 principal plus the final coupon of 500. The ledger-only audit measures this example on chain (check C6).

Amounts are checked against QuantLib and FinancePy: crates/bond_math/tests/reference.rs (fixtures generated by tools/reference/generate_fixtures.py) and, for every run, tools/reference/check_report.py.

4. Settlement flow

Each event (each coupon, and the redemption) has its own vault: a token account of the cash token whose authority is the Bond PDA. Cash in one vault can pay only that event.

Step Instruction Who What
1 create_bond registrar Mint (Token-2022, 0 decimals, extensions below), Bond account, transfer hook account list, in one transaction. Refuses cash tokens with transfer fees, transfer hooks, non-transferable extensions or a permanent delegate
2 register_holder registrar Holder registry: one HolderRecord per wallet per bond
3 place registrar Mints bonds to a registered holder, only before the first record moment and up to the issue size; refuses an issue whose budgets would not fit the cash token's u64 amounts
4 transfers holders Through the transfer hook (section 2)
5 open_event anyone, after the record moment Creates the event and its vault; budget from the supply at the record moment
6 funding issuer Cash into the event vault; simulated: the test bank mints it there (section 6)
7 confirm_funding anyone Vault >= budget -> funded; nobody is paid from an event that cannot pay everyone
8 pay_coupon anyone, any order, after the payment moment Position from the holder's history, amount from the terms; transfer from the vault and the holder's paid bit in one transaction; creates the holder's cash account if missing
9 redeem_holder anyone, after the redemption's payment moment Burns the holder's free bonds (permissioned burn) and pays their principal in one transaction
10 create_offer, funding, confirm_offer_funding issuer; confirmation by anyone Buyback or tender: price, maximum, window closing by the final record moment; own vault funded with price x maximum first
11 sell_to_offer holder Buyback: burn and pay in one transaction
12 change_tender holder Tender or withdraw during the window; tendered bonds stay in the holder's account, locked by the hook, so coupons at record moments inside the window still go to the holder
13 resolve_tender, settle_tender anyone, after the window Fix the accepted part and release the rest (no cash needed); then burn and pay the accepted part

Nothing depends on the operator. Steps 5, 7, 8, 9, 13 can be sent by any wallet at any later time; the amounts follow from the positions, so the sender cannot change them. Devnet run devnet-keeper: our operator stopped after coupon 0; a wallet with no role in the bond paid coupons 1-3 and redeemed everyone after the final record moment.

On-chain record of each action.

  • State: each holder's paid bit per coupon, position history, supply history, bonds burned at redemption and in offers, event and offer records with their vaults.
  • Program events in the transaction logs: BondCreated, HolderRegistered, BondsPlaced, PositionsSaved, EventOpened, EventFunded, CouponPaid, OfferCreated, OfferFunded, BondsBoughtBack, TenderChanged, TenderResolved, BondsRedeemed.
  • Token movements recorded by the runtime (pre/post token balances), which the ledger-only audit reads.

8. Limits

  • Bond types: fixed rate above zero; FixedPerPeriod and 30/360 only (no Actual/Actual, Actual/360, Actual/365); regular schedules (no stub periods); at most 40 coupon periods; direct holders only (no omnibus custodians).
  • The registrar is trusted to register holders and to place bonds up to the issue size before the first record moment; everything after registration can be done by anyone.
  • The cash token's issuer can freeze a vault or pause its token: payments stop until it acts again; nothing owed is lost (a_frozen_vault_pays_nobody_until_thawed).
  • Tender allocation: an oversubscribed offer buys exactly its maximum; which tenders receive a rounding bond follows the order the tenders were made.
  • The program's upgrade authority is a single wallet. A multisig or an immutable program, and any pause or intervention path, are governance decisions not yet made.
  • The audit checks the whole cash token (sum of balances = supply), which needs that token's full history. That fits a test token; for a widely used stablecoin the cash checks must be scoped to the bond's vaults.
  • Throughput: each payment of one coupon writes that event's vault. Measured on devnet (8-holder run, flow page, step Pay): one transaction paying 5 holders used 119,365 compute units, about 24,000 per holder paid, so with Solana's per-account limit per block (12 million units in the current cost model) one vault takes about 500 holder payments per block. The devnet runs were slower for a different reason: one operator sending one transaction at a time through the rate-limited public RPC.
