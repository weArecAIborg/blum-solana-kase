Blum services a tokenized bond on Solana, from issue to redemption

One Solana program keeps the bond's register and pays it. Each coupon goes to whoever held the bond at the record moment, even while the bond keeps trading. At maturity the program pays the principal and burns the bonds. The issuer can also buy bonds back. Everything in this deck ran on Solana devnet and links to its transaction.

3 · The problem

The bond keeps trading on coupon day. Who gets the coupon?

Kazakhstan's central depository takes the list of holders at 09:00 on the payment day and does not stop trading. Whoever held the bond at 09:00 is paid, whatever they hold when the money arrives. A Solana program cannot do that the same way: it only sees the accounts sent to it, so it cannot read every holder at 09:00.

Measured on devnet, 1000-holder run

BH#458 held 12 bonds at coupon 0's record moment. Right after it, BH#458 sold 3 of them to BH#43. Coupon 0 paid BH#458 on its 12 bonds, 600.00, and BH#43 on the 5 it held at the record moment, 250.00.

Across the run, paying by holdings at payment time instead would have got 1,039 of 4,000 coupon payments wrong.

4 · Record-date mechanism

Every transfer first writes down what each side held at the record moments that have passed

The bond is a Token-2022 token whose transfer hook calls the program inside every transfer. Before the bonds move, the program saves each side's quantity for every record moment that has passed since that holder's last change.

Between two changes a holder's quantity stays the same, so the saved number is exactly what the holder had at the record moment. A holder who never trades still has its record-moment quantity as its current one.

So there is no snapshot, no trading freeze, nobody has to act at 09:00, and nothing expires. Holders cannot change a balance any other way: only the program can burn, and Token-2022 refuses a transfer that skips the hook.

5 · Test tokenized instrument

The bond is a Token-2022 token: only the program mints or burns it, and every transfer runs through it

  • One token is one bond. The listing's example terms: face 1,000, 10% a year, paid twice a year, 2 years.
  • Transfer hook: every transfer runs through the program (slide 4).
  • Permanent delegate and permissioned burn: only the program can burn bonds, so a bond is never retired without its payment.
  • The program is the mint authority. It places bonds only with registered holders, only before the first record moment, and never above the issue size.
  • At creation the program checks the payment and record days against the terms, and refuses a cash token that could shrink or take payments (a transfer fee, a transfer hook, a permanent delegate).

6 · Holder registry

Only registered wallets can hold the bond

  • The registrar creates one holder record per wallet per bond. That record is the registration. Identity checks (KYC) are simulated: the registrar registers the wallets it is given.
  • The transfer hook refuses a transfer unless both sides have a holder record. Each holder uses one bond account, so one wallet has one position.
  • The holder record also keeps the holder's position history, its paid flags and the bonds it has tendered.
  • On devnet: 1,000 registered holders. Sending a bond to an unregistered wallet, and moving it to a second account of one's own, were both refused.

7 · Entitlement calculation

The amount is computed on chain from the terms and the position at the record moment

  • Coupon = bonds held at the record moment x face x rate / payments per year, in whole cash units, rounded down once per holder. A 30/360 day count is also supported.
  • The listing's example: 10 x 1,000 x 10% / 2 = 500. Measured: BH#1 held 10 bonds at the first record moment and was paid 500.00.
  • Principal = bonds x face x redemption price.
  • Each coupon's budget is the same formula on the whole supply at the record moment, rounded down once, so the payments never add up to more than the budget.
  • In the 1000-holder run, 4,000 of 4,000 holder-coupon amounts equal the terms. The bond math matches QuantLib and FinancePy.

8 · Scenario 1: coupon payment

Paid from the coupon's own funded vault, once per holder, by anyone

  1. open_event, by anyone after the record moment: creates the coupon and its own vault. The budget comes from the supply at the record moment.
  2. Funding, by the issuer: puts the cash into that vault (simulated: a test bank mints it there).
  3. confirm_funding, by anyone: the vault must hold the whole budget, or nobody is paid.
  4. pay_coupon, by anyone, in any order: the amount comes from the holder's record. The cash moves and the holder is marked paid in the same transaction, so nobody is paid twice.

Measured: 4 coupons to 1,000 holders, 2,003,300.00 in total, every vault emptied. Coupon 0 took 167 payment transactions of up to 6 holders each.

9 · Scenario 2: bond redemption

Principal paid and bonds burned in one transaction

  • Trading closes at the final record moment: the hook refuses any later transfer.
  • After the payment moment anyone can send redeem_holder. It burns the holder's bonds and pays their principal from the redemption vault, in one transaction. No bond is burned unpaid, and no principal is paid for a bond that still exists.
  • The final coupon is paid separately, like every other coupon.
  • Measured: 9,665 bonds from 918 holders redeemed, 9,665,000.00 paid, final supply 0. BH#1: 7 bonds burned and 7,000.00 paid in the same transaction.

10 · Scenario 3: the additional corporate action

Issuer buyback and tender offer

  • The issuer funds the offer's own vault with price x maximum before anyone can sell or tender.
  • Buyback, first come first served: the holder's bonds are burned and paid in one transaction. 1000-holder run: 96 sales, 176 bonds at 990.00.
  • Tender offer: holders tender or withdraw during a window. Tendered bonds stay in the holder's account, locked, so a coupon whose record moment falls inside the window is still theirs: 132 of 132 tendering holders were paid coupon 2 on their tendered bonds.
  • Oversubscribed: 441 tendered for a maximum of 410. Tenders are taken in the order they were made, each receiving its running pro-rata share, so exactly 410 were bought.
  • Example: BH#998 tendered 6, withdrew 1, had 4 of its 5 accepted and was paid 4,000.00.

11 · Verifiable on-chain record

Every step is a Solana transaction, and an independent audit re-checks them

  • Each step of the bond's life is a transaction anyone can open. The program logs an event for each one (BondsPlaced, CouponPaid, BondsRedeemed and others), and keeps paid flags and position history in its accounts.
  • The ledger-only audit reads only the public ledger and the run's scenario file. It never reads the program's accounts or the operator's report. It checks every balance and payment against the bond's terms, to the smallest cash unit.
  • 1000-holder run: 3,959 of 3,959 checks pass. Tamper test: sending 0.000001 cash into a vault from outside makes the audit fail.

12 · Nothing depends on the operator

Any wallet can finish the job, and none can change the amounts

  • Opening a coupon, confirming its funding, paying, redeeming and settling an offer can be sent by any wallet, at any later time. The amounts come from the positions on chain, so the sender cannot change who is paid or how much.
  • Keeper run on devnet: our operator stopped after coupon 0. A wallet with no role in the bond paid coupons 1 to 3 and redeemed every holder. The audit passed 23 of 23 checks.
  • Missed coupons never expire: they stay payable after later record moments.

13 · Attacks

35 attacks tried on devnet, 35 refused

  • At every step of the bond's life an attacker, and holders acting against the rules, tried to take bonds or cash or to act without authority: mint bonds, take another holder's bonds, pay a coupon twice, pay from the wrong vault, forge a position, trade after maturity, take over the program. Each refusal is a failed transaction anyone can open.
  • 21 more are refused in local tests.
  • Trusted, not stopped: the registrar, which registers wallets and places bonds; the cash issuer, which can freeze or pause its token (payments wait, nothing owed is lost); and the program's upgrade authority, a single wallet.

14 · Real-world applicability

Any bond: every market rule is a term of the bond

  • Record day and time, payment time, time zone, weekend, holiday calendar and coupon basis are terms of each bond, not code.
  • The sample follows the Kazakh depository: the list at 09:00 Almaty on the payment day, trading open on coupon day and stopped at redemption.
  • Payment dates come from the bond's holiday calendar. For the real KASE bond MUM108_0015, the computed dates equal KASE's published ones.
  • The program is the register and the paying agent: the issuer only deposits the cash.

15 · Implemented versus simulated

Everything on Solana is real; the cash rail, KYC and trading venue are simulated

16 · Technical overview, and how to check it yourself

  • Parts: the Solana program (Anchor), the bond math (schedules, day counts, amounts), the operator that runs a bond's life, a report check against QuantLib and FinancePy, the ledger-only audit, and the pages of this site, built only from measured devnet data.
